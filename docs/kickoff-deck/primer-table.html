Measured points

We park the robot at a few distances, change the RPM until balls go in, and write down what worked. In a real table the white rows are those measurements; these are example numbers.

In between: interpolation

At 2.5 m we are halfway between 2 m and 3 m, so we use halfway between 2080 and 2430. The blue row is worked out, not measured. The code draws a straight line between neighbouring rows.

A table beats a formula here

A formula needs the ball's weight, air drag and how much the wheel squeezes it. A table only needs real shots: park, shoot, write down what worked.

Example numbers, not measured on our robot. The real table comes from calibration night.

What RPM would you use for 3.5 m? About 2590, halfway between the 3 m and 4 m rows. Now imagine the camera is wrong by half a metre: you look up the wrong row, and the ball misses.
