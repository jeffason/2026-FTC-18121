Measured points

We park the robot at a few distances, change the RPM until balls go in, and write down what worked. The white rows are measurements.

In between: interpolation

At 2.5 m we are halfway between 2 m and 3 m, so we use halfway between 2080 and 2430. The blue row was never measured. The code draws a straight line between neighbours.

Why not a formula?

A formula needs the ball's weight, air drag and how much the wheel squeezes it. A table just needs a tape measure and an afternoon.

Example numbers from the simulator. The real table comes from calibration night on the robot.

Ask: what RPM for 3.5 m? Students should land on about 2590. That is the whole idea. Then ask what happens if the range measurement is wrong by half a metre: they look up the wrong row.
