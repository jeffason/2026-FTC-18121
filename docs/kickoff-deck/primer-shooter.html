How a flywheel shooter works

1

A motor spins a wheel

2

A ball touches the wheel

3

Faster wheel, longer throw

How far?

range filters

How fast?

table, then velocity modifiers

When?

fire gates

The spinning wheel is the flywheel. We tell its motor how fast to spin, in revolutions per minute. The feeder, the part that pushes balls into the wheel, pushes one in; the wheel grabs it and throws it, and slows down a little each time. The ball leaves at a speed set by the wheel, so a farther goal needs a faster wheel. That is the one relationship everything else is built on. Watch the wheel on the bench: spin it slowly and feed a ball and it falls short; faster and it goes farther. So the robot has three questions to answer. How far is the goal: range filters clean up the camera's distance. How fast must the wheel spin: a table turns distance into RPM, and velocity modifiers adjust it. Is now a good moment: fire gates can hold the shot until it is safe. Every piece of code you write for the shooter does one of those three jobs.
